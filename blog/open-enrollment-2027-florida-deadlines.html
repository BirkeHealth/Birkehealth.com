Florida Open Enrollment 2027: Enroll by Dec 15 for Coverage on Jan 1

Key dates, what to have ready, and what to do if you miss the deadline — in plain English.

By Birke Health Agency · Updated November 27, 2026

The short answer

If you live in Florida and want health insurance that starts January 1, 2027, pick a plan by December 15, 2026. You can still enroll until January 15, 2027, but then your coverage won't start until February 1. Either way, coverage only starts once you pay your first premium.

Every year, Open Enrollment is the main window to sign up for, renew, or change a Marketplace (Obamacare/ACA) health plan. Florida uses HealthCare.gov, so these are the dates that apply to you.

Key dates for 2027 coverage

DateWhat happens
Nov 1, 2026Open Enrollment starts. First day to enroll in, renew, or change a plan for 2027.
Dec 15, 2026Last day to enroll or change plans for coverage starting January 1.
Jan 1, 2027Coverage starts for people who enrolled by December 15 and paid their first premium.
Jan 15, 2027Open Enrollment ends. Last day to enroll or change plans for 2027.
Feb 1, 2027Coverage starts for people who enrolled December 16 – January 15 and paid their first premium.

Already have a Marketplace plan?

Open Enrollment is also your chance to review and change your current plan. Prices, doctors in the network, and covered medications can change from one year to the next — and so can your income or household. A few minutes of comparing now can save you money and surprises all year.

If anything has changed for you — a new job, a raise or pay cut, a new baby, a move — update your application so your savings are based on the right information.

What to have ready before you apply

HealthCare.gov's official checklist asks for the following. Gathering it first makes the application much faster:

  • Basic information for everyone applying: name, date of birth, and relationship to you.
  • Everyone in your tax household — usually you, your spouse, and anyone you claim as a dependent — even people who aren't applying for coverage.
  • Home and mailing addresses for everyone applying.
  • A Social Security number for each person, including those not applying. If you don't enter one, you may be asked for more information later.
  • Immigration document information for anyone applying who isn't a U.S. citizen or national.
  • How you file taxes — for example, married filing jointly or separately, and who you claim as a dependent.
  • Income information for everyone in the household: pay stubs or W-2s, tips, self-employment income, unemployment, Social Security, retirement income, and other taxable income.
  • Your best estimate of your 2027 household income. It's okay to estimate — you'll update it if things change.
  • Current health coverage anyone in your household has (Medicaid, Medicare, a job-based plan, etc.), including policy numbers.
  • Job-based coverage information for anyone who's offered a health plan through work, even if they don't use it.

How savings work

Many people pay less than the full price because of a tax credit that lowers the monthly premium. A few things to know:

  • Your savings are based on your estimated income for 2027 — the year you want coverage — not last year's income.
  • The amount depends on your household size and estimated income.
  • If you end up using more of the tax credit during the year than you qualify for, you may have to pay back the difference when you file taxes. That's why it's important to report income changes.

Missed the deadline?

After January 15, you can only enroll or change plans if you qualify for a Special Enrollment Period. That usually means you had a life event in the past 60 days, such as:

  • Losing health coverage — for example, losing a job-based plan, COBRA ending, or aging off a parent's plan at 26
  • Moving to a new zip code or county
  • Getting married, having a baby, or adopting a child

If you lost Medicaid or CHIP, you may have up to 90 days. And you can apply for Medicaid or CHIP at any time of the year.

Protect yourself from scams

Never share your personal information with anyone who offers you cash, gifts, or other perks to sign up. HealthCare.gov is the official Marketplace. A legitimate licensed agent will never pay you to enroll.

Frequently asked questions

What's the deadline for coverage starting January 1, 2027?

December 15, 2026. Your plan starts January 1 once you pay your first premium.

Can I still sign up after December 15?

Yes — until January 15, 2027. Plans chosen December 16 through January 15 start February 1.

What if I miss January 15?

You'll need a Special Enrollment Period, usually from a life event in the past 60 days (like losing coverage, moving, marriage, or a new baby). You can apply for Medicaid or CHIP any time.

Do I need a Social Security number for everyone?

The application asks for one for each person in your household, even those not applying. If someone doesn't have one or you don't enter it, you may be asked for more information later.

Does Florida have its own marketplace?

No. Florida uses HealthCare.gov, so the federal dates above apply.
