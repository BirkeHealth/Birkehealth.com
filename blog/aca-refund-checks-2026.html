The $500 ACA Refund Checks: What We Know So Far

Who qualifies, how the checks are sent, what's still unclear, and what it means for your 2027 coverage.

By Birke Health Agency · Updated November 27, 2026

The short answer

The White House says nearly 1 million Marketplace enrollees in 30 states — including Florida — will get a refund check of $500 per person. The checks are meant for people who did not receive premium assistance (they paid the full premium), and mailing began in early October 2026. What's still unclear is how the checks are treated for taxes. One thing hasn't changed: in Florida, choose a plan by December 15, 2026 for coverage starting January 1, 2027.

If you've seen headlines about "Obamacare refund checks," you're not alone. Several of our clients have asked us whether they'll get one, and whether it changes their plan or their taxes. Here is a calm, plain-English look at where things stand.

What the administration announced

On September 10, 2026, the White House published a fact sheet announcing the "Working Families Obamacare Refunds." According to the announcement:

  • Amount: $500 per person.
  • How many people: nearly 1 million Americans.
  • Where: the 30 states that use the federal exchange (HealthCare.gov), including Florida. People in states that run their own exchanges are not included.
  • Why: the administration says Marketplace "user fees" collected in past years were higher than needed to run the federal exchange, and it is returning the surplus.
  • Timing: checks to begin going out in October 2026.

Who qualifies

The White House says the refunds go to Marketplace enrollees who do not receive premium assistance — in other words, people who paid the full monthly premium without a premium tax credit.

ABC News, citing an administration official, reported on October 1, 2026 that most recipients have incomes above 400% of the federal poverty level, and that some people earning between 100% and 400% who did not get subsidies are also included. The official said Florida has the second-highest number of recipients, after Texas.

If you received a premium tax credit to lower your monthly payment, the announcement suggests you are not part of this refund.

How the checks are sent

According to ABC News, the Treasury Department is issuing the checks by mail, starting the week of September 30, 2026, and each check comes with a letter. The administration hasn't said how long delivery will take. The announcement does not describe any application, so watch your mail rather than looking for a form to fill out.

What's still unclear

  • Taxes. We haven't found official IRS guidance on whether the payment counts as income.
  • Exactly which years and plans count. The announcement doesn't spell out which coverage years are used to decide who qualifies.
  • Delivery timing. There's no official estimate of when every check will arrive.

We'll update this article as more official information is published.

How to check for yourself

  • Read the official announcement on WhiteHouse.gov (linked in the sources below).
  • Watch your mail for a Treasury check and the letter that comes with it.
  • Log in to your HealthCare.gov account to see whether you received premium assistance, and to check your messages and notices.
  • Watch IRS.gov for any guidance about taxes.
  • Keep the letter and a record of the payment. If tax questions come up later, you'll want them.

Watch out for refund scams

Any time money is in the news, scammers follow. Be very careful with calls, texts, or emails that promise to "release" or "speed up" your ACA refund. Don't pay a fee, and don't give out your Social Security number, bank details, or HealthCare.gov password to someone who contacts you out of the blue. When in doubt, hang up and go directly to HealthCare.gov or IRS.gov yourself.

Does the check affect your 2027 plan?

The refund is described as a one-time payment back for past fees. We haven't seen any official information saying it changes your Marketplace plan or your 2027 savings. What we do know is that Open Enrollment dates are unchanged:

  • December 15, 2026 — last day to choose a plan for coverage starting January 1, 2027.
  • January 15, 2027 — Open Enrollment ends. Plans chosen December 16 through January 15 start February 1.

Either way, coverage only starts once you pay your first premium. Don't let refund news distract you from the deadline — waiting on a check is not a reason to skip renewing or comparing plans. For the full list of dates and what to have ready, see our guide to Florida Open Enrollment 2027.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to apply for the $500 refund check?

The White House announcement does not describe any application. According to the administration, the Treasury Department mails the checks with a letter. Never pay anyone or share personal or bank information to claim a refund.

Will I get a check?

According to the White House, checks go to Marketplace enrollees in 30 federal-exchange states, including Florida, who did not receive premium assistance. If you got a premium tax credit, the announcement suggests you are not included.

How much is the check?

$500 per person, according to the White House.

Does the check change my 2027 plan?

We haven't seen official information saying it does. The deadline is still December 15, 2026 for coverage starting January 1, 2027.

Is the check taxable?

We haven't found official IRS guidance yet. Keep your letter or payment record and ask a tax professional when you file.

Someone called offering to speed up my refund. Is that real?

Be very cautious. Don't pay a fee or give out your Social Security number, bank details, or account password. Go directly to HealthCare.gov or IRS.gov instead.
