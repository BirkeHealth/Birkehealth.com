The $500 ACA Refund Checks: What We Know So Far

What's been reported, what still needs official confirmation, and what it means for your 2027 coverage.

By Birke Health Agency · Updated November 27, 2026

The short answer

News outlets, including ABC News, report that the Trump administration has begun mailing $500 ACA refund checks. We have not yet confirmed the official details — who qualifies, when checks arrive, or how they're treated for taxes — so check HealthCare.gov and IRS.gov before acting. One thing hasn't changed: in Florida, choose a plan by December 15, 2026 for coverage starting January 1, 2027.

If you've seen headlines about "Obamacare refund checks," you're not alone. Several of our clients have asked us whether they'll get one, and whether it changes their plan or their taxes. Here is a calm, plain-English look at where things stand.

What's been reported

On November 26, 2026, ABC News published a story titled "Trump administration begins mailing out $500 ACA refund checks. Here's what to know." Other national outlets have covered the same checks.

That's the core of the news: the administration says it is sending $500 checks connected to ACA (Marketplace) coverage. Beyond that headline, we're being careful. News stories have described possible eligibility rules, but we haven't been able to confirm those details on an official government page yet, so we're not repeating them here.

What we haven't confirmed yet

Before you count on a check — or tell a family member they'll get one — these questions need official answers:

  • Who qualifies. Not every Marketplace customer may be eligible.
  • How much per household. The reported amount is $500, but we haven't confirmed how that applies to families with more than one person.
  • When checks arrive and how they're sent.
  • Taxes. We haven't found official IRS guidance on whether the payment counts as income.

We'll update this article as official information is published.

How to check for yourself

The safest place for official answers is the government itself:

  • Log in to your HealthCare.gov account and check your messages and notices. Florida uses HealthCare.gov, so that's where any Marketplace notice would appear.
  • Watch IRS.gov for any guidance about taxes.
  • Check the HHS and CMS newsrooms, where the agencies that run the Marketplace post official announcements.
  • Keep any letter or payment record you receive. If tax questions come up later, you'll want it.

Watch out for refund scams

Any time money is in the news, scammers follow. Be very careful with calls, texts, or emails that promise to "release" or "speed up" your ACA refund. Don't pay a fee, and don't give out your Social Security number, bank details, or HealthCare.gov password to someone who contacts you out of the blue. When in doubt, hang up and go directly to HealthCare.gov or IRS.gov yourself.

Does the check affect your 2027 plan?

We haven't seen any official information saying these checks change your Marketplace plan or your 2027 savings. What we do know is that Open Enrollment dates are unchanged:

  • December 15, 2026 — last day to choose a plan for coverage starting January 1, 2027.
  • January 15, 2027 — Open Enrollment ends. Plans chosen December 16 through January 15 start February 1.

Either way, coverage only starts once you pay your first premium. Don't let refund news distract you from the deadline — waiting on a check is not a reason to skip renewing or comparing plans. For the full list of dates and what to have ready, see our guide to Florida Open Enrollment 2027.
