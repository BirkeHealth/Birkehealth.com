Medicare and Immigrants: Who Could Lose Coverage on February 1, 2027

A new federal law changes who can have Medicare. Here's who is affected, what the notices mean, and what to do — in plain English.

By Birke Health Agency · Updated November 27, 2026

The short answer

A federal law signed July 4, 2025 limits Medicare to U.S. citizens and nationals, green card holders, Cuban and Haitian entrants, and COFA migrants. People on Medicare with other immigration statuses — for example, people granted asylum, refugees, or parolees who aren't Cuban and Haitian entrants — could lose Medicare on February 1, 2027 unless they show Social Security they qualify. Social Security checks are not affected.

If you or someone in your family is on Medicare and is not a U.S. citizen, this change matters. Social Security has started mailing notices, and the official rule was finalized in November 2026. Here's what it says.

Who can keep Medicare

Under the new law (section 71201 of Public Law 119-21), you can only have Medicare if you are in one of these four groups — and you still have to meet Medicare's usual age, disability, or other rules:

  • U.S. citizens or U.S. nationals
  • Lawful permanent residents (green card holders)
  • Cuban and Haitian entrants
  • People who live in the U.S. under a Compact of Free Association (COFA migrants)

Who could lose Medicare

Before this law, many "lawfully present" immigrants could get Medicare. Now, people with other statuses can't. The final rule lists, for example, people granted asylum, people admitted as refugees, and people paroled into the U.S. for at least a year as categories that used to qualify as lawfully present.

Not sure whether your status counts — for example, whether you're considered a Cuban and Haitian entrant? Don't guess. Contact Social Security and ask them to check your records.

Key dates

WhenWhat happens
Nov 2026Social Security sends preliminary notices — about 300,000 — to people who may not meet the new rules.
Dec 2026Formal termination notices go to people who haven't shown they qualify. Medicare (CMS) also plans to send a notice, with help available in 14 languages.
Feb 1, 2027Medicare coverage ends for people who were on Medicare as of July 4, 2025 and don't meet the new rules.

Medicare's actuaries estimate about 32,000 people will actually lose coverage — far fewer than the number of notices. Many people who get a notice may simply need to update their records.

Got a notice? Do this now

  1. Read it carefully. A preliminary notice means Social Security's records show you may not qualify — it is not the final decision.
  2. Contact Social Security (SSA) as soon as possible if you believe you qualify, and bring updated proof of your citizenship or immigration status.
  3. Fix your immigration records if they're out of date — the rule notes people may need to correct records with immigration authorities (USCIS).
  4. Call 1-800-MEDICARE if you have questions about your coverage.
  5. Know your rights: you can appeal a decision that ends your Medicare.

Your Social Security check is not affected

This change is about Medicare only. According to the final rule, people who receive Social Security retirement or disability benefits will keep receiving them, even if their Medicare ends.

Protect yourself from scams

News like this attracts scammers. Social Security and Medicare will not ask you for money to "keep" your Medicare. Only share your information with Social Security, Medicare, or a licensed agent you trust — never with someone who calls you out of the blue.

Frequently asked questions

Who can keep Medicare under the new law?

U.S. citizens and nationals, lawful permanent residents (green card holders), Cuban and Haitian entrants, and people living in the U.S. under a Compact of Free Association (COFA), as long as they meet Medicare's usual rules.

When does coverage end for people who don't qualify?

For people who were already on Medicare on July 4, 2025 and can't show they qualify, Medicare coverage ends February 1, 2027.

Will I lose my Social Security check too?

No. The change is about Medicare only. According to the final rule, people who get Social Security benefits keep receiving them even if their Medicare ends.

What should I do if I get a notice?

Read it right away and contact Social Security (SSA) with updated proof of your status if you believe you qualify, before your coverage ends. You can also call 1-800-MEDICARE. You have the right to appeal.
