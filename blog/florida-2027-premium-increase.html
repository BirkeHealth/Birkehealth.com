Florida Marketplace Premiums Are Rising for 2027. Here's How to Compare Before December 15

The sticker price is going up, but what you actually pay depends on your income, your household, and the plan you pick.

By Birke Health Agency · Updated November 28, 2026

The short answer

Florida's insurance regulator shows individual health plan premiums rising an average of 10.4% for 2027, from $811 to $898 a month per person before tax credits. Your real cost depends on your income, household, and plan, so compare plans before December 15 instead of letting yours renew automatically.

You may have seen headlines that Marketplace (Obamacare) premiums are going up again. For Florida, that's what the state's official numbers show. But the headline number isn't your bill. Here's what it means and what to do.

What Florida's numbers show

The Florida Office of Insurance Regulation (OIR) publishes average monthly premiums for every company selling individual ACA plans in the state. For 2027:

ItemWhat the state reports
2026 average$811 a month per person, before tax credits
2027 average$898 a month per person, before tax credits
Average change+10.4%, weighted by enrollment
By companyChanges for companies on HealthCare.gov range from about 6% to 25%

Two things to know about these numbers. First, they don't include tax credits, which lower the price for many families. Second, the state says they are averages, not the change for any single person, and that they haven't been finalized by the federal government yet, so they could still change.

Why your price may be very different

Many people who buy through HealthCare.gov get a premium tax credit that lowers their monthly payment. How much you get depends on your household size and your estimated 2027 income. That's why two neighbors with the same plan can pay very different amounts.

The federal government (CMS) says more than 75% of people who buy through HealthCare.gov will still be able to find a plan for $50 a month or less in 2027. Whether you're one of them depends on your situation, and the only way to know is to compare.

One important tip: report your income as accurately as you can. If you get more tax credit during the year than you qualify for, you may have to pay the difference back at tax time.

Don't just let your plan auto-renew

If you already have a Marketplace plan and do nothing, HealthCare.gov will automatically re-enroll you for 2027 so you don't lose coverage. That's a safety net, but it isn't a review. Your renewed plan could cost more, and your savings will be based on the information already on file unless you update it.

Insurance companies also change or end plans from year to year. If yours is ending, you'll get a letter, and you may be moved to a different plan with the same company, or a comparable plan with a different company. That can mean a different network of doctors.

A 5-step check before December 15

  1. Update your 2027 income estimate and household on your application.
  2. Compare your monthly premium after savings, not the sticker price.
  3. Check your doctors and hospitals are in the plan's network.
  4. Check your prescriptions are on the plan's drug list.
  5. Look at the deductible and out-of-pocket maximum, not just the monthly price.

Frequently asked questions

How much are Florida health insurance premiums going up for 2027?

Florida's Office of Insurance Regulation shows an approved average increase of 10.4% for individual plans, from $811 to $898 a month per person, before any tax credits. It's an average across all companies, so your own change can be higher or lower.

Will I pay the full price?

Not necessarily. If you qualify for a premium tax credit, it lowers your monthly premium. The amount depends on your household size and estimated 2027 income. HealthCare.gov shows your price after savings when you compare plans.

What happens if I do nothing?

If you have Marketplace coverage, HealthCare.gov will automatically re-enroll you in a plan for 2027 so you don't have a gap. But it may not be the best price for you, and it may be a different plan if yours is ending.

What if my insurance company stops offering my plan?

You'll get a letter. HealthCare.gov may move you to a different plan with the same company, or to a comparable plan with a different company. Check that your doctors and medicines are still covered.

What's the deadline to switch plans?

Choose a new plan by December 15, 2026 for coverage that starts January 1, 2027. Open Enrollment ends January 15, 2027. Plans picked from December 16 to January 15 start February 1.
